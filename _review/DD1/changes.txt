## purple-sun
  before: Handhooking in mixed fibres — wool, cotton, silk, acrylic, and recycled cotton.
  after:  Handhooking in mixed fibres. About a week of near-daily work. Made as a cushion for Stine's office chair; it now belongs to Ulrikke.

## birds
  before: A mixture of tufting and embroidery on canvas. Wool and cotton, with acrylic painting. Two birds.
  after:  A mixture of tufting, embroidery and painting. Two birds.

## fuck-alting
  before: "Fuck everything" in Danish, stitched by hand. The tone is not angry — it is tired and specific. The slowness of embroidery makes the message stranger and more serious.
  after:  "Fuck alting" — Danish for "fuck everything" — embroidered by hand. The small embroidered pieces shown below it are part of the work.

## gud-har-meldt-afbud
  before: "God has cancelled." One of the earliest text works. Made during a period when it felt exactly true. The stitch count is several hundred. Saying it this slowly changes what it means.
  after:  "Gud har meldt afbud" — God has cancelled. Hand embroidery. There are more of them. More graphic than Gud Har Meldt Afbud II. Shown together with Gud Har Meldt Afbud II and Long Hair Don't Care as three standalone works.

## elsk
  before: The Danish word for love — "elsk" — stitched in hand embroidery and nothing else. No decoration. No frame of reference. Just the word, made slow, given weight.
  after:  The Danish word "elsk" — "love" — embroidered by hand.

## be-a-dragon
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on fabric
  before: An instruction in hand embroidery on canvas. Not "be like a dragon" — the command is simpler than that. The invitation is open. What it means depends on who reads it.
  after:  A quote from Game of Thrones, embroidered by hand.

## theres-nothing-here
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on watercolour-painted fabric
  hidden: False  ->  True
  before: A statement stitched on fabric. It might be a refusal or a description — the text genuinely does not decide. Read it fast and it is dismissive. Read it slowly and something opens up.
  after:  Hand embroidery on watercolour-painted fabric. Not finished yet — the photograph is a process shot. Hidden from the site until it is finished and photographed.

## mariann
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on fabric
  availability note: -  ->  Not for sale — hangs at Stine's parents'
  before: A name stitched as portrait. To embroider a name is to insist it is here. Mariann exists in this piece in a way that does not require explanation or justification.
  after:  One of a pair of embroideries made as 70th-birthday gifts — one for Stine's mother, one for her father. They hang at her parents' home.

## 38-homes
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on fabric
  before: Freehand embroidery — marks that follow their own logic without trying to arrive anywhere. Not sketches for something else. These lines are the destination.
  after:  One work in about 15 parts. Stine counted every home she has lived in and made one piece per home. The parts belong together as one unit, and the work is open-ended. Only some of the parts are shown here.

## collage-bw
  title: Collage (Black & White)  ->  Collage
  medium: Hand embroidery on linen, monochrome thread  ->  Hand embroidery on fabric
  before: Hand embroidery on fabric in monochrome. 100×70 cm — the largest embroidery work. Collage logic in thread: cut from one reference, placed against another, the seams visible by design.
  after:  Made at night, after waking up. A thread collage in monochrome and dark blue.

## apple-scraps — REMOVED, redirect -> /works/fuck-alting
  before: Apple Scraps | embroidery | Domestic remnants embroidered on fabric. The apple core, the peel, the ordinary aftermath of a meal. Embroidery is a practice that dignifies things — it takes time, and time makes objects matter.

## perfidt-perfekt
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on fabric
  before: "Perfectly perfidious" in Danish — the two words cancel each other and yet land together. Hand embroidery on canvas. A sentence from an argument Stine was in and couldn't let go of.
  after:  "Perfidt perfekt" — hand embroidery on fabric. The last word is deliberately left unfinished: "perfekt" is not completed, so the work itself is imperfect.

## ingenting
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on fabric
  before: "Nothing" in Danish, stitched. The word for absence given material form. There is something satisfying about spending several hours making the word nothing. It resolves a contradiction.
  after:  A detail of an embroidery reading "Her er ingenting" — "There is nothing here" in Danish.

## gud-har-meldt-afbud-ii
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on cotton fabric painted with colour
  before: The second version of "God has cancelled." The same sentence, differently stitched. One version was not enough — the phrase needed to be made twice to understand what it actually said the first time. A repetition that is not redundancy.
  after:  Hand embroidery on cotton fabric painted with colour. The text is being overtaken by flowers: God can't be bothered any more, now it is up to us. The message is hidden, grown over organically. More floral than the first, which is more graphic. Shown together with Gud Har Meldt Afbud and Long Hair Don't Care as three standalone works.

## long-hair-dont-care
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on a hand-embroidered tablecloth found at a flea market
  before: Hand embroidery on canvas. A phrase that refuses to apologise for itself — not a political statement so much as a position. Took longer to stitch than to say. The time changes what it means: something casual made slow becomes something else.
  after:  Embroidered on top of a hand-embroidered tablecloth found at a flea market. The point is swearing on top of someone's careful handwork. Shown together with the two Gud Har Meldt Afbud pieces as three standalone works.

## fireworks
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on fabric — sampler
  before: Five embroidered starbursts in gold, crimson, and lavender thread, scattered across deep teal fabric. Satin stitch radiating from a tight centre — the stitch direction does the same job a firework's trail of sparks does.
  after:  A sampler (prøvestykke) for trying out embroidery techniques. Five starbursts in gold, crimson and lavender thread, scattered across deep teal fabric, worked in satin stitch.

## det-er-bare-tanker
  medium: -  ->  Hand embroidery on vintage floral fabric, framed
  availability note: -  ->  Not for sale — with Ulrikke
  before: Hand embroidery on vintage floral fabric, framed. Danish for "it's just thoughts" — stitched in a fading rainbow gradient, yellow into red. The busy printed rose fabric underneath does the opposite of quieting the text: it makes the thought feel like it interrupted something already in progress.
  after:  Hand embroidery on vintage floral fabric, framed. "Det er bare tanker" — "it's just thoughts" — is a phrase from a psychologist, used against intrusive thoughts. Stitched in a fading rainbow gradient, yellow into red. Made for Ulrikke when she went to efterskole; Ulrikke has it.

## red-to-blue-broderi

  after:  Made on Strynø, inspired by the sea and the sunset. Embroidery on cotton fabric dyed with watercolour and ink. A wave in beads, sequins and thread breaks across one corner; loose French knots are scattered across the dyed field.

## universe-1

## universe-2
  before: Acrylic and oil stick on canvas. A different attempt at the same question as Universe I. Deeper ground colour, more layers scraped back. The question the series asks doesn't change — the answers keep changing.
  after:  One of the three paintings in the Universe series. The photograph is a process photograph.

## universe-3
  before: Acrylic and oil stick on canvas. Third iteration. A darker palette than the first two — more purple, less ground visible. Worked on the studio floor; you can see the direction of the marks.
  after:  One of the three paintings in the Universe series. The photograph is a process photograph.

  availability note: -  ->  Temporary work — no longer exists

## red-and-green-moss

## no-ordinary-stone

## taped-objects

## flowers-on-linen

## on-the-light-table

## dead-flowers
  before: Dried flowers photographed close up. After the bloom there is a different kind of beauty — drier, more structural, more architectural. This is a photograph of what remains. Archival inkjet print.
  after:  A series over time: every bouquet Stine picked was allowed to wither and was photographed. More photographs exist. Stine wants these as large prints.

## purple-flower

## colourful-shadows
